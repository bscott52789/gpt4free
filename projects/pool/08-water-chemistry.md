# 08 — Water chemistry for a salt pool

## Targets

| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Free chlorine (FC) | 2–4 ppm (3–5 if CYA is 70+) | The only number that keeps swimmers safe |
| Combined chlorine | < 0.5 ppm | Higher means chloramines; run BOOST or shock |
| pH | 7.4–7.6 | Salt cells drive pH **up** constantly; expect to add acid |
| Total alkalinity | 60–80 ppm | Lower than a traditional pool to slow pH rise |
| Calcium hardness | 150–250 ppm (vinyl liner) | Too high scales the cell and heat exchanger; too low is harmless to vinyl |
| Cyanuric acid (CYA) | 50–80 ppm | Salt pools run higher CYA so FC survives sun |
| Salt | 3200–3800 ppm, ideal 3600 | Per the iChlor label |
| Phosphates | < 500 ppb | Algae food; matters only if algae is a recurring fight |
| Metals (Fe, Cu) | 0 | Test fill water once |

## Testing

- Weekly minimum in season: FC, pH. Monthly: TA, CH, CYA, salt.
- Use a drop‑based kit (Taylor K‑2006C or TFTestkits TF‑100 with the salt add‑on).
  Strips are fine for a quick glance, not for dosing decisions.
- The iChlor's salt number is an estimate derived from conductivity and temperature;
  it reads low in cold water. Trust the drop test.

## Rules of thumb per 10,000 gallons

| To change | Add |
|---|---|
| pH down ~0.2 | ~12 oz muriatic acid (31.45%), poured slowly in the deep end with the pump running |
| TA down 10 ppm | ~26 oz muriatic acid, then aerate to bring pH back up |
| FC up 1 ppm | ~10 oz of 10% liquid chlorine (for emergencies; the cell handles normal demand) |
| CYA up 10 ppm | ~13 oz stabilizer in a sock hung in front of a return |
| Salt up 100 ppm | ~8.3 lb pool salt |
| CH up 10 ppm | ~1.25 lb calcium chloride |

Working volume is ~19,500 gal (18 x 36, 3–6 ft), so multiply each dose by **1.95**, or just double it for rough work.

## The salt‑pool pH cycle

Electrolysis produces hydrogen gas at the cell, and the bubbling drives off CO₂, which
raises pH. So the routine is: test pH weekly, add a measured dose of muriatic acid,
keep TA on the low side so the drift is slow. A 1 gal jug of acid per month in summer
is typical for this size pool.

## Shocking

A salt pool rarely needs granular shock. On a vinyl liner, never let undissolved granules touch the floor. Options in order of preference:
1. BOOST on the iChlor for 24 hrs of pump time.
2. Liquid chlorine (10–12.5%) to raise FC to about 40% of CYA.
3. Avoid cal‑hypo (adds calcium) and trichlor pucks (adds CYA and acid) except as a
   short‑term winter supplement.

## Water balance and the heat exchanger

The UltraTemp's titanium exchanger tolerates salt water, but scale from high pH and
high calcium still coats it and cuts efficiency. Balanced water protects the most
expensive component on the pad.
