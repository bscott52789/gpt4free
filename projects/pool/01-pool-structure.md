# 01 — Pool structure

Source: 46‑second walk‑around video, 2026‑09‑08. Frames saved in `photos/pool-*.jpg`.

## What the video shows

- **Shape**: rectangle with a wide shallow shelf at one end and molded features. The
  smooth, uniformly colored light‑blue surface with radiused inside corners and molded
  steps/bench is characteristic of a **one‑piece fiberglass shell**. Confirm with the
  builder paperwork; a vinyl liner would show seams and a plaster pool would show
  trowel texture, neither of which appears.
- **Entry**: corner entry steps with a stainless handrail on the shallow end
  (`pool-entry-steps-handrail.jpg`, `pool-corner-steps.jpg`).
- **Shallow shelf / bench**: a broad shelf across the shallow end and a molded bench
  running along one long wall (`pool-shallow-end-bench.jpg`).
- **Deep end**: two circular drain covers on the floor, i.e. **dual main drains**
  (`pool-deep-end-drains.jpg`). Dual drains spaced apart are the Virginia Graeme Baker
  Act configuration that prevents body entrapment. Do not replace covers with anything
  other than VGB‑compliant covers of the same size.
- **Skimmer**: at least one white square skimmer lid in the deck on the long side
  (`pool-skimmer-lid-deck.jpg`). Check for a second lid; the video pans past white
  squares in two locations.
- **Returns**: small wall fittings visible along the walls. Count them on a calm day
  and note which is closest to the equipment pad.
- **Light**: a bright spot on the deep‑end wall in several frames suggests one
  underwater light. Its circuit is the 20 A single‑pole "Pool Lts + Rec" breaker.
- **Coping and deck**: light travertine‑look coping pavers with a gray paver deck.
  Pavers on sand will shift slightly over time; keep polymeric sand in the joints.
- **Fence**: black aluminum picket fence, wooded lot beyond. Pine needles and pollen
  are the main debris load.

## Dimensions and volume

Owner recollection (2026‑09‑08): 16x32, 18x32, or 18x36 ft; 3 ft shallow end to 6 ft
deep end, not counting the three entry steps and the tanning ledge.

Rectangle volume ≈ length × width × average depth × 7.48. Average depth with a 3‑to‑6
slope is 4.5 ft. Steps, bench, and tanning ledge displace water, so knock roughly 10%
off the rectangle number.

| Candidate | Rectangle gal | With ~10% off for steps/ledge/bench |
|---|---|---|
| 16 x 32 | 17,200 | **~15,500** |
| 18 x 32 | 19,400 | **~17,500** |
| 18 x 36 | 21,800 | **~19,600** |

**Working number: 17,500 gal** until measured. Chemical doses in
`08-water-chemistry.md` are per 10,000 gal, so multiply by 1.75.

How to settle it in five minutes: tape from inside edge of coping to inside edge of
coping, both directions, at the waterline. Then check the depth at the deep‑end drains
with the pole.

**A useful tell on construction:** one‑piece fiberglass shells are almost never wider
than 16 ft because of highway transport limits. If the tape says 18 ft wide, the pool
is very likely a vinyl liner with liner‑covered steps and ledge, not fiberglass. That
changes the care rules: a liner must never be drained, is punctured by sharp objects
and by chlorine pucks lying on the floor, and should have its water kept balanced to
avoid wrinkles and bleaching. If it says 16 ft, the fiberglass notes below apply.

## Fiberglass shell care (if confirmed)

- Never drain the shell without a professional. An empty fiberglass pool can float or
  bulge from groundwater, and the wooded lot suggests a high water table after rain.
- Keep calcium hardness in the 150–300 ppm range and pH 7.2–7.6 to protect gelcoat.
- Avoid abrasive cleaners and steel brushes; use a nylon brush.
- Metal staining (brown/black spots) points to iron or copper in fill water. Test the
  hose water once.
