# 12 — Seasonal plan

## September to mid‑October (shoulder season)

- Water drifts down from the low 80s. Decide the target (82–84 °F) and heat only in
  afternoons with the cover on every night.
- Pine needles increase. Empty baskets twice a week.
- Lower pump run hours as water cools; algae pressure drops below ~70 °F.
- Chlorine demand drops; cut the iChlor % as FC creeps up.

## Late October to November (closing)

Two options in central Virginia:

**Option A: keep it open year‑round.** Common with fiberglass and a variable‑speed
pump. Run the pump at low speed continuously whenever air is forecast below 32 °F
(moving water does not freeze in the pipes). The heat pump must still be **drained**
or bypassed and its plugs pulled for freeze protection per the manual. The iChlor
stops below ~52 °F water, so add a small amount of liquid chlorine weekly or a trichlor
puck in the skimmer.

**Option B: winterize.**
1. Balance water: pH 7.4–7.6, FC 3–5, TA 70–90.
2. Clean the pool thoroughly. Backwash, then set the multiport between positions
   (WINTER).
3. Lower water to just below the returns for a fiberglass pool only if the builder
   says so; many fiberglass builders want the water kept full. **Ask the builder.**
4. Blow out the lines from the pad with a blower or compressor, plug returns and
   skimmer (gizmo), pour non‑toxic pool antifreeze into skimmer lines.
5. Drain the pump (both plugs), filter (bottom drain), heat pump (drain plugs, both
   headers), and the iChlor cell (remove the cell, store indoors).
6. Remove the pressure gauge and store it indoors.
7. Turn off breakers 1/3 and 4/6 at the subpanel. Leave the light circuit if you
   want winter lighting.
8. Cover with a safety cover or a mesh cover; a tarp with water bags is the fallback.

## March to April (opening)

1. Remove cover, clean and store dry.
2. Reinstall plugs, gauge, cell, and drain plugs. New lid and union o‑rings if
   cracked. Lube everything with silicone.
3. Fill to mid‑skimmer. Prime the pump, bleed the filter.
4. Run the filter 24 hours, then test everything and rebalance. Salt reads low in
   cold water; wait for 60 °F before adjusting salt.
5. Turn the heat pump on for the first time on an afternoon above 55 °F and confirm
   warm discharge air and a rising outlet temperature.
6. Reset the START pointer on the gauge to the clean pressure at your normal speed.

## Freeze nights any time

If a hard freeze is forecast and the system is open: run the pump continuously, open
the bypass and the heater valves so water moves everywhere, and confirm the heat pump
has either drained or has water flowing through it. Standing water in the titanium
exchanger cracks the headers.
